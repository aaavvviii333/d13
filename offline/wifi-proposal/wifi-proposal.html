01 · Executive Summary

DOE filtering blocks core staff tools

Citywide Zscaler filtering on the DOE network blocks the development and productivity tools our programs are built on.

GitHub Pages · blocked Claude · blocked ChatGPT · blocked Vercel · block expected Mullvad & other advanced VPNs · block expected
A VPN is not a long-term solution
DOE blocks VPNs by policy ("Attempting to gain access to material that is blocked or filtered by the Department;" DOE Acceptable Use Policy).
Basic VPNs are all blocked. Only high-security TLS-encrypted ones slip through, and only because they aren't on the block lists yet.
02 · Policy Compliance

Separate network, separate people, no conflict

Compliance position
NYC FIRST staff are not DOE employees and receive no DOE funding or salary. Running a separate cellular gateway, outside DOE infrastructure, breaks no departmental rule.
Mandatory guardrail
Students must never connect to this network.
Student tools, activities and devices stay on approved DOE infrastructure, which keeps municipal student access fully separate from staff operations. Enforce it with a staff-only SSID, a non-shared passphrase and a periodic review of the client list in the UniFi console.
03 · Physical Architecture

No cabling, heavy RF noise, thick concrete

Facilities allows no cabling of any kind: no drilling, no runs through walls, ceilings or floor slabs, and not even a temporary cable taped along a wall or stairwell. All equipment sits together in one spot at the stairwell threshold. NYC FIRST has used cellular workarounds before, and earlier feasibility work on 5G extenders for the basement dead zone points to the same answer: a gateway placed at a threshold, with nothing installed in the building.

−65 dBm is about the weakest signal that can reliably feed a mesh point. −70 dBm is the minimum for usable staff Wi-Fi. Mesh roughly halves throughput through the UX7, which is still more than the 5G connection delivers. The UX7 plugs into the outlet on the landing wall. The landing is part of the fire stair, so confirm with facilities that a small plug-in device is allowed there. If the double doors are steel fire doors that stay closed, they block a lot of signal: survey B with the doors shut.

07 · Cisco Donation

Option 4: applying through TechSoup

Cisco donates Meraki gear to eligible nonprofits through TechSoup. We pay only TechSoup's admin fee, but the licenses expire, so this option has a recurring cost the UniFi options don't.

Confirm before applying
Organizations affiliated with a parent that has more than 20 affiliates are excluded. Ask TechSoup whether a FIRST Regional Partner counts.
Also excluded: Cisco Networking Academy providers, and anyone who bought Meraki gear in the past 6 months.

Yes. There's no lifetime license. In year 5 we renew through TechSoup at that year's fee, and eligibility is checked again.
